# Contract and story

## The contract (top of every storyboard.md)

```
Thesis: one sentence, the source's own claim.
Argument chain: 3–6 steps the viewer must walk through, in order (problem → diagnosis → move → payoff).
About X, not Y: the misreading this film must rule out.
Budget: seconds per step; which steps may show screens; where the music's drop lands.
Ship gate: the blind reader's thesis matches the one above.
```

Why it exists: on Walk to Digest v2 three reviewers each made reasonable cuts (drop the diagnosis for pacing, promote the anecdote to the hook, linger on the edit), and the film stopped arguing what the article argued. Every reviewer note and every director note is weighed against the chain: **a cut to the chain is a scope change that needs the user, not a pacing fix.**

## Chains by source type

**Article / essay.** Use the author's own argument, in the author's order: problem → diagnosis → the move → how to do it → payoff (the rule, the title's promise) → honest handoff ("Still an experiment" if the piece is an experiment). The title's promise usually belongs on the last card. Anecdotes are illustrations inside a step, not the hook, unless the source is a story.

**Inbox / notes digest.** Find the one thesis the items share; say it as "about X, not Y". Each item becomes a scene or a "not used, because…" row in the storyboard; never force an item into the chain. If the items genuinely share nothing, say so and ship the strongest two or three.

**Product / launch film.** Problem the user has → the product doing its job in its real UI (screenshots, real components, real copy) → one proof moment → CTA. Only claims the product can show, plus any truthfulness rules the house notes set for that product. A product's brand beats the house style.

## Storyboard variants

Before building, write three beat sheets that differ in structure (not in colour): e.g. cold open on the source's most concrete moment; a single continuous camera move; a split screen of the two worlds. One line of tradeoff each, then pick. The beat sheet is in bars at the chosen track's tempo, with the caption per bar.

## Captions

- The source's own words wherever possible; close trims are fine ("Every open loop is one click away" from "At a computer, every open loop is one click away"). Record the trims in the storyboard.
- Up to ~8 words, one line in the caption band. The kit lints any caption wider than the frame (`LINT:` on every render); fix it rather than shrinking the font below the format's size.
- Keep the source's voice, including its "I"/"you" switches; don't hedge or sharpen its claims.
- Never show a number the source doesn't prove. Third parties' posts are anonymised and paraphrased; drop crude ones.
- Speech in the scene (a quoted phrase, a transcript line) goes in the scene as speech, not in the caption band: a caption reads as narration, and a half-finished caption reads as a mistake.

## Screen/time budget

Say where the time goes and check it against the thesis. A film about "less screen time" that spends most of its runtime on screens argues against itself; the blind reader will notice. The step that carries the thesis gets the most time and the music's drop.
