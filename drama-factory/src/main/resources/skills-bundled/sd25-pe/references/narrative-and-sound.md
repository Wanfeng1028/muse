# Narrative, camera, and sound

## Story and timing

Convert prose into filmable events, retaining cause, reaction, relationships, key dialogue, and outcome. A trailer can use montage; a requested scene keeps its continuous events. Do not reduce an explicitly requested whole story to one scene. Without a stated scope, choose a coherent event and make it evident in the prompt.

Use shot numbers, sequential stages, or useful timestamps. The supplied guide permits all three; timestamps are not reserved for requests already containing them. When designing timing, use continuous whole-second intervals and realistic density. Avoid subsecond repetition quotas such as three head shakes per second. Preserve explicit user intervals unless retiming is authorized.

The guide describes single outputs up to 30 seconds. For longer requested stories, provide connected clip prompts or identify the conflict instead of silently squeezing the story. Settings stay outside the prompt; shot timing inside describes creative events.

If an external duration and user timeline disagree, do not silently strip the timeline. Retime when requested or when the duration override is explicit; otherwise flag incompatible hard constraints. Stretch existing actions/reactions before inventing new plot. For handoffs and physical processes, state each end state and the single object's ownership.

## Performance and camera

Express emotion through a few readable cues tied to its trigger. Broad action summaries often work better than exhaustive limb choreography; expand distinctive beats. Preserve intentional stillness. Near-misses or simulated injuries must not become actual injury.

Use familiar camera terms directly. Explain specialized effects visibly: rack focus shifts sharpness between subjects; dolly zoom changes perspective while retaining subject size. Keep target, direction, start/end framing, and transition triggers clear. Avoid contradictory camera commands.

## Speech, music, and text

Preserve supplied dialogue/lyrics/signage and punctuation unless translation/rewriting is requested. Write these strings, including newly composed lines, in their intended spoken or displayed language; the English prompt rule does not translate them. A quoted fragment is not permission to invent the rest. Compose dialogue when asked; preserve inaccessible source-speech instructions without fabricating words.

Bind turns to speaker, language, delivery, and audio role. Distinguish speech from voiceover. Do not replace requested speech with silence or arbitrary mouth movement. Do not infer regional accent from script or identity. Distinguish music, effects, and dialogue so removing one preserves the others.

The original local skill suggests these optional writing delimiters; they are not established API syntax in the supplied guide. Natural prose also works:

| Content | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Music | `(Soft piano music)` |
| Effect | `<A bell rings>` |
| Dialogue | `{Hello}` |
| Subtitle | `【Departure】` |

With angle brackets for effects, use ordinary character names instead of angle-bracket subject tags. Do not change exact supplied punctuation just to adopt delimiters. Exclude subtitles, dialogue, BGM, or all sound when requested, not as an automatic pack.
