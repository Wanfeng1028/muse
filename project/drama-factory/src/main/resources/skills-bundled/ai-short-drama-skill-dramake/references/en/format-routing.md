# Format Routing

## Standalone microshort: 15–45 seconds

Use one strong visual promise, one protagonist-specific attempt, one escalation, and one payoff. A series may share characters and style without requiring previous episodes.

## Standalone short: 45–120 seconds

Use 2–4 story blocks. The viewer should understand setup, desire, obstacle, escalation, result, and final button without a synopsis.

## Anthology or unit episode

Keep recurring characters, abilities, relationships, wardrobe, and location rules. Each episode introduces and resolves one external problem. Carry personality and state, not unresolved exposition.

Recommended episode contract:

- familiar cold-open behavior or world rule;
- episode-specific problem;
- a protagonist trait, relationship, ability, limitation, or product function creates both trouble and solution;
- consequence or relationship shift;
- repeatable ending ritual.

## Serialized drama

Maintain a season ledger for relationships, knowledge, injuries, props, abilities, and open questions. Every episode must still have a local objective and payoff. Do not use a cliffhanger as a substitute for a complete scene.

## Selection rule

Choose the shortest form that delivers the premise. If the conflict resolves in two beats, make a short. If characters make a meaningful choice and consequences persist, consider a unit or serial episode.

## Aspect ratio

Support `9:16`, `16:9`, `1:1`, and custom ratios. Record channel, subject-safe area, caption area, cover crop, and delivery codec.

Landscape and portrait versions share story and continuity canon but receive separate composition, shot size, motion path, safe-area design, cold-viewer review, and technical QA. Do not default to center-cropping or mechanical padding.
