# Previsualization, coverage auditions, and frozen keyframes

A video model should not make the first decision about cast identity, spatial layout, and hero composition. Previsualization turns the most drift-prone expensive-generation decisions into inspectable still facts.

## Three visual-asset layers

1. **Long-term masters:** character turnarounds and expressions, wardrobe topology, recurring props, location maps, and scale relationships. They control who and what exist and where.
2. **Coverage auditions:** two to four shootable alternatives for the same story moment, changing only scale, camera, blocking, or occlusion. They are choices, not video shots that must all be generated.
3. **Frozen keyframes:** approved first, last, state, or composition frames that directly constrain a request. They control how a moment starts or lands.

A multi-pose character sheet controls identity only; it must not create duplicate characters. A location map controls layout, not labels, white backgrounds, or its overhead composition. Record `controls` and `must_not_transfer` for every reference.

## When previsualization is required

Do it before paid video generation when any of these apply:

- two or more recurring or visually similar characters;
- a recurring prop, wardrobe state, text, or logo affects the plot;
- precise blocking, eyelines, occlusion, scale, giant scale, or spatial continuity matters;
- first or last framing must be exact;
- risky contact, chase, transformation, reflection, asynchronous mirror action, or a multi-space one-take;
- a recognizable real location is part of the audience promise.

Simple reaction shots, abstract montage, or reliable live-action first frames may skip coverage auditions, but still require identity and medium anchors.

## Coverage acceptance

Review silently: what the audience sees first, what the subject must do, where the danger or gag enters, whether the action path is occluded, why the camera moves, and whether the ending hands off to the next block. Prefer the clearest causal geometry with the lowest action load; spectacle does not replace readability.

For live-action or photoreal work, also check real-location perspective and motivated light, subject scale, ground contact, traversable paths, background-character roles, and safe areas. Giants need visible scale anchors such as lamps, buildings, vehicles, cabins, or people.

## Keyframe rules

- If a first frame owns composition, do not let a conflicting style reference compete for it; the same applies to last frames.
- Whether reference-to-video can coexist with strict first/last frames comes from the live schema. Choose one control target when they are mutually exclusive.
- One-takes favor location maps, identity masters, and accepted short-term state; multi-shot blocks freeze only meaningful landing frames.
- Repair identity, wardrobe, prop, or spatial errors in a frozen frame before video generation.
- Record source, version, approval status, control duty, and dependent block for every frozen frame.
