## Hooks and retention

**A hook is three tracks written separately**:

| Track | Job | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Picture (0 to 3 s) | stop the thumb | motion or a face in frame 1; the strongest image, never a logo |
| Spoken line | open the loop | 5 to 12 words in the viewer's language |
| On-screen text | anchor muted viewers | ≤ 6 words; complements the line, never repeats it word for word |

The first seconds confirm the promise of the title or thumbnail, open a question the viewer can't close alone, and show
that the payoff exists. A mismatch between promise and opening makes viewers feel lied to and leave.

**Recognition ladder** (score the hook on its first ~5 words alone): the viewer's own behaviour > named audience > a
concrete number or stake > founder origin (weak for direct response) > a generic question or statement ("Have you ever…"),
which never scores well.

| Formula | Example (fictional) |
|---|---|
| The viewer's own moment | "It's 11 p.m. and you're rewriting the same invoice reminder." |
| Named audience | "Freelance designers: this one's about getting paid." |
| Stated cost | "Forty days. That's how long my last client took to pay." |
| Result first | [invoice marked PAID] "This got paid in two days. Here's the email that did it." |
| Before/after in one cut | [dead plant → same pot, new leaf] "Same plant, same window. Only the watering changed." |
| A checkable mistake | "If your reminder starts with 'just checking in', that's why they ignore it." |
| Contrarian with proof | "Everyone says water weekly. My sensor said day nine, then day four." |
| Searchable question | "How do you ask a client for money without sounding rude?" |
| Clock | "Two minutes, and your handover sheet is done." |
| Experiment with a stake | "I let a sensor decide when to water for a month." |
| Countdown | "Three lines every invoice reminder needs." |
| Visual spectacle | [a plant "speaking" through a phone notification] |
| Narrow direct address | "If your kid fights bedtime reading, watch this." |
| Gentle recognition (sensitive) | "Her classmates are reading. She isn't yet. That's okay." |

Write 3 to 5 candidates from different rows, all three tracks each, then choose. Curiosity needs a closeable gap: give a
partial answer ("the email that got me paid had one line most people leave out"), not a blank ("you won't believe this").

**Anti-hooks** (rewrite on sight): greetings, logos, "In this video", "Welcome to", "Introducing"; "Imagine…", "What if I
told you", "In a world where", "Have you ever wondered"; cheap conspiracy ("nobody talks about this"); a stat without a
source or stakes; a hook the video never pays off; a hook about the brand instead of the viewer; a pivot from the hook to
an unrelated pitch (seconds 3 to 15 must extend the hook's premise).

**Retention**
- Open loops: open a question, pay it off, open the next before the previous closes. One loop stays open until the reward.
- A stakes line in seconds 2 to 7: why this matters to the viewer.
- Re-hooks (new information, a turn, a visual change) roughly every 5 to 8 s in short-form; pattern interrupts every 2 to 4
  s (practitioner heuristics, not laws).
- Pay the hook's promise in the last 20%, but show early that the payoff exists (a glimpse of the receipt at second 5).
- Signpost topic changes ("step two is where most people go wrong"); unsignposted changes are where viewers leave.
- A steady bleed through the middle is pacing: cut, don't rewrite.
- Loopable ending: the last line sets up the first. Never end on "follow for more" over a fade. One ask, one CTA.
- Long-to-short clip selection: score each self-contained 15 to 60 s window 1 to 10 on hook strength, quotability, emotional
  intensity, self-containedness and surprise; pick non-overlapping clips with varied topics; get approval before rendering.
