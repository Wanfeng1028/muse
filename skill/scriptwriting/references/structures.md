## Framework chooser

Starting points; compound when needed (`SB7 with PAS inside the problem`).

| Format | Length | Goal | Structure | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bumper ad | 6 s | recall one idea | single image + twist + brand (see "Ads by length") | room for one idea; build for 6 s, never trim a 30 |
| Social ad, cold audience | 15 s | stop and click | hook → pain → proof → CTA (compressed PAS) | pain earns attention fastest with strangers |
| Social ad, warm audience | 15 to 30 s | convert | BAB or 4Ps | they know the pain; show the after and prove it |
| TV / pre-roll | 30 s | brand + sales | ABT mini-story, brand early | room for one character and one turn |
| Brand / manifesto film | 45 to 90 s | belief | golden circle with a story spine | belief first; a human story avoids the sermon |
| Launch / promo film | 45 to 90 s | announce + desire | SB7, or the keynote reveal for one hero feature | customer as hero; the reveal builds anticipation |
| Product demo | 30 s to 3 min | "I get it, I want it" | demo loop inside BAB | show the job done, not the menu |
| Concept explainer | 60 s to 8 min | understanding | SCQA, then escalation | question-led; earns attention by curiosity |
| Product explainer | 60 to 120 s | understanding + action | SB7 or SCQA → three-step plan → CTA | clarity beats cleverness; a plan reduces risk |
| Tutorial | 30 s to 10 min | competence | outcome first → steps → check → recap | show the end result first |
| Founder story | 60 to 180 s | trust | story circle, hero handed to the customer | a need, a price paid, a change |
| Testimonial / case study | 30 to 120 s | proof | who → before → trigger → doubt → result → advice | the doubt makes it believable |
| Interview / documentary | any | truth, emotion | paper edit onto a story circle or ABT | you find the story; you don't impose it |
| Organic short-form | 15 to 45 s | watch-through, shares | hook → retain → reward → loop | retention is the main signal |
| LinkedIn video | 30 to 90 s | credibility | SCQA or ABT, text-led | professional, often muted feed |
| Trailer / teaser | 15 to 150 s | anticipation | cold open + three acts + button | start strong, pull back, build, withhold |
| Music montage | 15 to 60 s | mood | three-act energy arc on the track's sections | the track's form is the structure |
| Changelog | 30 to 60 s | "worth trying now" | feature reveal × n, marquee first | lead with the change most users care about |
| Sensitive audience (parents, health, money) | any | relief, trust | PAS with "acknowledge" instead of "agitate", or SB7 | name the worry, comfort it, show a doable step |

Choose by the viewer's **awareness** (Schwartz's ladder, as a heuristic): unaware of the problem → start with the problem
(PAS, SCQA, story); problem-aware → the pain in their words; solution-aware → the difference (BAB, comparison);
product-aware → proof and offer (4Ps, testimonial).

## Frameworks

Timings assume ~2.5 words/s. Examples use fictional products: **Tally** (invoicing for freelancers), **Sprout** (soil
sensor for houseplants), **Porter** (shift handover for nurses).

### ABT: And, But, Therefore (Randy Olson)
AND (agreed context) → BUT (conflict) → THEREFORE (consequence, the answer). The test for any script, scene or line;
standalone for 15 to 30 s spots and LinkedIn. Over 90 s, stack ABTs (each "therefore" becomes the next "and"). Failure
modes: "and, and, and" (a list) and "despite, however, yet" (too many turns).
Timings: 15 s = 0–4 / 4–9 / 9–15 (incl. CTA); 30 s = 0–8 / 8–16 / 16–30.
Example (Tally, 15 s): "You finished the logo on Friday, and the client loved it. But it's day 40 and the invoice is
still unpaid. So Tally sends the reminders for you, politely, until it's paid."

### Story spine (Kenn Adams; Pixar)
Once upon a time… / Every day… / But one day… / Because of that… (×2 or 3) / Until finally… / And ever since then…
For brand films with a character, 60 to 90 s promos, founder stories. Not for 6 to 15 s spots or tutorials. "Every day"
must be specific; "because of that" must be causal, never "and then"; "ever since" is where the product's value lives.
Timings: 60 s = 0–12 / 12–18 / 18–40 / 40–50 / 50–60; 90 s = 0–15 / 15–24 / 24–62 / 62–76 / 76–90.

### Story circle (Dan Harmon) and the compact hero's journey
1 YOU (comfort) → 2 NEED → 3 GO (unfamiliar situation) → 4 SEARCH → 5 FIND → 6 TAKE (pay a price) → 7 RETURN → 8 CHANGE.
For founder stories, documentaries, case studies, 90 s+ brand films. Under 30 s, merge to need → go → find/take → change.
**Step 6 is what commercial scripts skip and why they feel fake**: the hero pays something (time, money, pride, a belief;
in a testimonial, the doubt or the switching cost). Compact 60 s hero's journey: ordinary world → call (the problem) →
mentor (the product as guide) → threshold (first use) → ordeal (the hard moment, honestly shown) → reward → return.

### SCQA and the pyramid (Barbara Minto)
SITUATION (what the viewer agrees with) → COMPLICATION (what changed) → QUESTION → ANSWER (the claim) + 3 supporting
points. For explainers, B2B, LinkedIn, investor and internal videos. Not for emotional brand films. The question is often
the hook: open on it, then back-fill S and C in a line each.
Timings: 60 s = S 0–6, C 6–14, Q 14–18, A + points 18–54, close 54–60.

### Escalating explainer (inferred from studios like Kurzgesagt)
Open question with personal stakes → ground the basics in human-scale images → escalate scale or complexity step by step,
each step a new image system → a twist or uncomfortable implication → back to human scale with an honest landing. Carry
one metaphor through; make numbers physical; humour as relief between hard ideas; a source sheet (`facts.md`). It teaches;
it doesn't sell.

### StoryBrand SB7 (Donald Miller)
A CHARACTER (customer) wants something → has a PROBLEM (external, internal, philosophical) → meets a GUIDE (empathy, then
authority) → who gives a PLAN (usually 3 steps) → and CALLS TO ACTION → avoiding FAILURE → ending in SUCCESS. For product
explainers, launch films, landing-page videos. The internal problem (how it feels) sells; the external one justifies. Keep
the labels internal or it reads like a template; keep failure stakes gentle for sensitive audiences.
Timings: 60 s = character 0–6, problem 6–20, guide 20–28, plan 28–42, CTA 42–48, success 48–60.

### PAS: Problem, Agitate, Solve
Name the problem in the viewer's words → make its cost felt → product as the way out, with proof. For cold audiences with
an urgent known pain. For sensitive audiences swap Agitate for **Acknowledge**: show you understand, then relieve.
Timings: 15 s = 0–3 / 3–7 / 7–12 / CTA 12–15; 30 s = 0–4 / 4–12 / 12–25 / CTA 25–30.

### BAB: Before, After, Bridge
Life with the problem → life without it → the product as the bridge. Strongest when before and after are the same shot,
same framing, one variable changed. Variant: AFTER first as the hook. Not when the after can't be shown honestly.

### AIDA, 4Ps, golden circle
- **AIDA** (attention, interest, desire, action) is a checklist of the buyer's mind, not a story; add a "but" between
  interest and desire or it goes flat.
- **4Ps** (picture, promise, prove, push) for product-aware and offer-led spots; the prove beat must be real footage or
  sourced data. 30 s = 0–7 / 7–12 / 12–25 / 25–30.
- **Golden circle** (why → how → what) for brand, manifesto and recruiting films. A why must exclude someone to mean
  anything; pair it with a story spine so the belief is shown in one life.

### Hook, retain, reward (short-form)
HOOK (first 1 to 3 s) → RETAIN (a chain of small open loops, each paid off as the next opens) → REWARD (the promised payoff,
then out fast, ideally into a loop). 30 s = hook 0–3, stakes 3–7, 3 to 5 retain beats 7–25, reward 25–29, loop line 29–30.

### Keynote reveal (as described by Carmine Gallo)
Villain or status quo → one headline line → rule of three → demo → a saved "one more thing" turn → the headline again.
For one hero product or feature, 60 to 120 s; not for multi-feature updates or when there is no real surprise.
90 s = 0–12 / 12–18 / 18–60 / 60–75 / 75–84 / 84–90.

### Trailer and teaser (Derek Lieu)
COLD OPEN (strongest intriguing moment, no context) → ACT 1 (world, who) → ACT 2 (conflict, escalation) → ACT 3 (montage to
music, best lines) → TITLE → BUTTON (one beat after the title). Start strong, pull back, build; contrast makes the build
land; show the unique feature early. Teaser = cold open + one question + title.
60 s = 0–6 / 6–20 / 20–38 / 38–52 / title 52–56 / button 56–60.

### Documentary and interview: finding the story
Transcribe everything; mark lines with emotion, a fact, a turn or a quotable phrase; paper-edit selected bites before
touching the timeline; map them onto a story circle or ABT; find the turn (the moment the subject says something they
didn't expect to); open on a self-contained bite that raises a question, end on one that answers or reframes it. Write VO
or cards only where footage can't bridge. **Never splice sentences into a meaning the speaker didn't say.**

### Testimonial and case study
WHO (one line) → BEFORE (situation and cost, their words) → TRIGGER (why now) → DOUBT (what almost stopped them) → FIRST
RESULT (a moment) → RESULT (a sourced number) → ADVICE (to someone like them). Keep the doubt beat; marketing teams cut it
and lose credibility. Never script the customer's words: interview, then select. 60 s = 0–5 / 5–18 / 18–30 / 30–50 / 50–60.

### Founder story
The founder's own problem on a specific day → attempts with existing options and why they failed → the build (the price
paid) → the first person it helped → the customer as hero → invitation. Hand the hero role to the customer halfway; a
founder film that stays about the founder is a vanity film.

### Tutorial (Diátaxis principles)
OUTCOME first (the finished result in the first 5 s) → what you need → steps, one action each with the visible result
("you should now see…") → check → one-line recap + next step. No theory mid-procedure. 60 s = 0–5 / 5–8 / 8–52 / 52–60.

### Screenwriting rules for short films
- Every beat turns a value (`+` to `−` or back). Same value at both ends: cut the beat or fold its information elsewhere.
- Enter late, leave early. Know the ending first; the last image and line decide every earlier beat.
- Opening and closing images mirror each other: the same frame, changed.
- Each setback teaches the hero something new; two setbacks teaching nothing new become one.
- Endings feel inevitable yet unexpected. Never resolve with coincidence: the customer acts, the product enables.

## Ads by length

One message per spot, whatever the length. Longer spots carry more proof and more feeling, not more messages.

| Length | Can carry | Beats | VO words | Template |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 s | one idea, one image, brand | 2 to 3 | 0 to 12 | 0–2 striking visual or twist; 2–4.5 the line; 4.5–6 brand + sting |
| 15 s | one problem, one proof | 4 | 25 to 35 | 0–3 hook; 3–6 pain; 6–11 proof; 11–15 brand + CTA |
| 30 s | one character, one turn | 5 to 7 | 55 to 75 | 0–3 hook; 3–8 setup; 8–12 but; 12–22 proof/demo; 22–26 payoff; 26–30 brand + CTA |
| 60 s | a mini-story, two proofs | 8 to 12 | 110 to 140 | 0–5 hook; 5–15 world; 15–22 but; 22–30 turn; 30–48 proof ×2; 48–54 payoff; 54–60 CTA |
| 90 s | a full arc, emotion + proof | 12 to 18 | 150 to 200 | story spine or SB7 timings; leave 10 to 20% as music or silence |

- 30 s has room for exactly one "but"; two buts are two ads. 60 to 90 s can carry a person's story and sags without one.
- The CTA promises an outcome, not just an action ("Get paid this month: try Tally free" beats "Try it free"). Voice the CTA
  and show it.
- Final check (Google ABCD): Attention (jump in, tight framing), Branding (early, throughout, also in audio), Connection
  (humanise, one message, humour, surprise), Direction (a clear, voiced CTA).
